Illness Is Not a Punishment

Illness was never atonement for sins, a punishment, or the price of doing something bad — after all, plenty of bad people never get sick! These are mistaken thought patterns, sadly inherited from family and society, with no basis in truth. Illness can change a lot in your life, but it doesn’t necessarily change your worth, erase your identity, or take away your right to love, to be loved, and to enjoy life — even while you’re still in treatment.

Why We Keep Testing the People Who Love Us

Relationships are the space that exposes every trauma that has shaped us — the kind you might think about and say, “No, I don’t think what happened to me is the reason.” It may well turn out to be. Constantly putting others through tests of their patience and their love is nothing but a fear of rejection and a fear of an old pain repeating. That pain came from unstable treatment by a father or a mother: the hours when dad was home were tense and your nervous system was on alert; treatment that swung with his mood that day; threats of abandonment; emotional manipulation; blame; intimidation through religion and punishment — and other parental behaviours that left us afraid of rejection and of being hurt again. So when you finally find a safe space, or simply a different one, you keep testing how long it will hold. Yet this is what normal looks like; you just need to understand that it was the old that wasn’t normal. Instead of reinforcing it with behaviours in your present, go back and work on the old. It’s time to experience healthy relationships. 🍃

Cheating Was His Choice — Not Your Fault

So many women are cheated on and then blame themselves for it. A woman often believes the betrayal rests on her own shortcomings in the relationship, but it is far bigger than a shortcoming. First, there’s the illusion of control: accepting a false “truth” that I caused it gives my mind an explanation — however shallow — that it can digest. Second, some women carry a negative self-image, so of course they are the cause of everything, they are bad, and they deserved it. Sometimes some of them stay after the betrayal because they don’t want to be alone, or because of an unhealthy attachment to the person. Worse still is society itself, whose first message to a woman after infidelity is: “Look at what you did to make him look elsewhere.” Then there’s the smaller society — her own family, how she was raised and what she saw at home: a father cheating on a mother, a brother cheating on his wife, or a collective belief that this is normal! Cheating isn’t about what I did; it’s about what he chose to do. Even if some marriages have conflicts and shortcomings, they are no justification for cheating!

When? To Whom? How Far?

Let’s start from the very beginning. Every one of us has a little child inside — spontaneous, carefree, free, never overthinking. Sometimes reckless, sometimes falling, sometimes laughing and playing. In times of overflowing feelings, love and relationships, it’s this child who moves us — the one who can’t calculate or speak the language of logic. And just as there’s a child within us, there’s also a parent and an adult who advise us, guide us, and tell us no. Sometimes we chase after that child without thinking. Besides, as a girl, what you did back then was a way of expressing love, safety and many similar feelings. But there’s a lesson to learn too: when, to whom, and to what extent did it serve its purpose? Simply put, the point isn’t the mistake — it’s learning from it and growing. Being kind to yourself lets the process finish in peace, while whipping and blaming yourself sinks your feet deeper into the pain, and you won’t learn the lesson… My advice to every girl: before any step, answer these — When? To whom? And how far?

Why He Disappears the Moment You Lean In

Many young men fall for a girl, express their love, show interest and are constantly present — and the moment she shows she’s on board, or shows interest and love… he runs. There are many reasons, but the most common are:

1) He isn’t sure of his feelings for you.

2) He was just passing time and things got serious.

3) He’s afraid of responsibility.

4) He has harmful traits and manipulative behaviours.

5) Sometimes winning you over is just a challenge to him.

6) He has a psychological problem or a mental illness.

7) Family trauma, like parents fighting in front of their children.

And sometimes a young man is so tangled inside that he doesn’t even know why he does it… Some stay in relationships for years but, deep down, are unable to marry or carry responsibility, so they vanish whenever they feel pressured. One more possibility: if none of the above applies, maybe the relationship between you is tense, with unresolved problems, and he chose to withdraw instead of looking for solutions.

Leave Without Revenge

One of the common mistakes girls and women make after a breakup, or after being hurt by the other person, is the urge for revenge — or “to prove something to him before I leave.” She writes a tight scenario and believes it will play out exactly as imagined, but halfway through comes a bigger pain because of that decision. Instead of healing, we keep digging into the wound. And truthfully, even the goal behind it — getting a message across or proving a point — doesn’t happen; the opposite happens, and she keeps digging deeper into the pit of revenge and anger, until some people lose themselves completely and lose the ability to rise again. My advice: if you realise you have to leave, leave without wanting revenge, justice or to prove anything. Save your energy for yourself — life has its own way of proving everything.

Compare Yourself Only to Yourself

Comparison will always make what you have look small, simply because you’ll be comparing against what you lack, not the whole picture. If you want to compare, compare yourself to who you used to be: how you’ve grown, what you’ve learned, what experience you’ve gained, what you’ve taken from it… Comparing yourself to others is a lie.

“Just Until the Right One Comes Along”?

One of the tricks we fall for is writing a justifying scenario and believing it will unfold exactly as planned, without considering the real reasons behind the decision: a wish to regulate emotions I can’t regulate on my own — loneliness, the need to feel valued, and needs for attention and care I don’t know how to handle — so I decide to meet them through a relationship, thinking I can simply walk out of it as soon as the right person shows up. Let me tell you, it doesn’t work that way at all. First, if you can’t handle yourself and your own needs, you’ll never be in a balanced relationship. Second, how will the right person arrive when I’m not yet right myself? Isn’t there work to do on yourself so you can understand what a real relationship means? Third, who says I won’t get attached to the relationship I’m in now, or do things I’ll regret, or stay stuck, unable to leave?… All these words and scenarios, my dear, are an excuse from your mind, because it thinks it will stay in control and everything will be fine!

Why Some People Dread Their Birthday

When someone hates a special day like this, there’s usually more than one reason behind it. First, comparison between how people and the circles around them celebrate and how they celebrate. Then there’s facing a new year of their life, which reminds them the days and years are passing while they stand still. Or the day is the anniversary of disappointment — no one remembers it, celebrates it, or does anything nice for them; it’s just a day like any other… Or a third reason entirely: the day carries a specific bad memory, and every time it comes around they relive those painful feelings. Another part: some people are still trapped in the past — in days of warmth and love with certain people beside them — so when the day comes and they’re still locked in the past, the wound opens again. What they share is the escape: I’ll avoid celebrating, I’ll sleep and switch my phone off until the day is over. Yet it isn’t about a day; it’s about wounds still inside us that we think are over once the day ends.

Never Single for Long?

Some people can’t stay out of a relationship — and sometimes they aren’t even aware of it. There can be more than one reason: a fear of loneliness, not knowing how to meet their own needs for attention, encouragement and companionship except through a partner, or a history of turbulent relationships or a hard childhood that affected their self-worth. That sometimes leads them into many wrong relationships and choices. They need to understand why they can’t be without a relationship, make a decision, and start meeting their needs in healthy ways without depending on someone else.
